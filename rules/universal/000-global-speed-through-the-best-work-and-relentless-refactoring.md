# What all of this is for: the project's speed over months and years

The user measures speed over the whole project, across months and years, not by a turn, a task or a session. In his
words (2026-11-21): "I'm looking for global speed. The project as a whole over month and years. My experience tells me
that happens through the most competent coders doing the best possible work at all times + relentless refactoring. Our
whole process is set up around this belief."

Every rule here serves that. Where a rule's wording doesn't settle a case, this does:

- **The best possible work, every time.** Models run at their full ability, at the effort setting that gives the best
  results: "I'm looking for the optimal results, not the cheapest usage." Cost is kept acceptable by other means, such as
  more subscriptions, never by doing worse work. That doesn't mean the most expensive model: don't choose Fable 5.2
  without strong reason unless the user asks for it. He doubts it outperforms Opus 5.5 at programming, and it costs twice
  as much (2026-11-23).
- **Relentless refactoring.** What is learned goes back into the code, the tools and the rules at once: see "The
  codebase must improve over time, never degrade" in the [collaboration rules](001-collaboration-rules.md).
- **Local speed is judged by this measure.** A shortcut that saves a turn and leaves the code, the tools or the rules
  worse makes the project slower. A slower turn that leaves them better makes it faster.
