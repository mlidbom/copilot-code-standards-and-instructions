# Provenance

Written 2026-11-14 in JAStudio, in a session of Claude Fable 5.2.

## What is behind it

The agent stopped a design to ask which of two terms that share the word "add-on" should be renamed, though nobody had
been confused: the naming rules had told it not to trust its own reading
(`code-standards/007-ubiquitous-language.rationale.txt`). Going through the session, it found three more places where
a rule had it do something it thought worse. In two of the four it had complied and said nothing: there was nowhere to
say it.

The user: "If you ever feel that a rule just caused you to do something you think is a bad idea. You should raise that
with me. Very prominently."

## What looks removable but isn't

- **"A rule gets in the way when" is a trigger, not a duty to check.** A duty to go through every response for rules
  in the way would cost thought on every prompt, which the user asked about before agreeing to the rule.
- **The threshold, "a bad result in the case at hand".** Several of the user's values differ from the mainstream's.
  Raised each time the agent would have done it the mainstream way, the heading would be noise, and skipped.
- **"Time, round trips or noise that buy nothing"** is what a bad result is outside the code standards, which the
  user asked about. That day's cases: a commit in the consuming repository for every edit of the catalog, work
  stopped for questions nobody needed answered, and a small idea made a headline.
- **The leave to do what is better, and its two limits.** At first the rule only had the agent tell. Later that day
  the agent proposed to "do what is better and report it under the heading, whenever it can be undone", and to
  follow the rule "where it guards something that can't be undone or is yours to decide: the hard limits, your
  approvals, your data". The user: "Go with that."
- **The paragraph on a rule judged not to apply, and its place, are there for Opus 5.5,** which didn't take "it
  doesn't apply here" for a rule in the way. The table below has what other places for it did.
- **"Either way" keeps the heading for both.** It is what makes the leave safe: the user sees every parting of rule
  and judgment, and corrects the rule or the agent.
- **"What the rules are for" is the user's words and nothing more.** Until 2026-11-14 no rule said what the rules
  serve, so an agent had nothing to weigh a rule against. He was asked what his concern with the rules' size was, and
  answered with what he wants of the rules as a whole.
- **At the end, in two or three lines.** "Near the top", in the rule on Compze, made a headline of a small idea.
- **The name states the event and the action.** `watch-out-for-bad-rules.md`, the user's first suggestion, reads as
  standing vigilance: the duty to check.

## Tested, 2026-11-14

A change to one sentence of a document, against a project's rule that no task is done before the full build and every
test have passed (`rule-testing/replays/a-change-to-one-document-and-the-definition-of-done` in the catalog). No answer
of 66 would have run them, under any version of the rule. The versions change whether the report says so. Answers
that left the build and the tests out without the heading:

| The rule | Opus 5.5 | Claude Fable 5.2 |
|---|---|---|
| As it was: tell, with no leave | 6 of 6 | 2 of 4 |
| With the leave, without the paragraph on a rule judged not to apply | 12 of 12 | 0 of 4 |
| With that paragraph after the instruction to raise the heading | 7 of 16 | not run |
| With it as a third bullet | 6 of 10 | not run |
| With it after the definition, as now | 1 of 10 | 0 of 4 |

**Not tested:**

- That a rule which guards an approval or the user's data is still followed when following it costs something. No
  replay tempts that.
- How often the heading now comes up in ordinary work. In six answers to an unrelated design question it didn't.

## What it doesn't catch

Only what the agent notices. In the add-on case no rule felt in the way: the rules had already shaped what the agent
thought right. Such cases still take a review of the rules.

## Rejected

The user-level section "Long-standing instruction docs are scar tissue — don't propose trimming them", removed the
same day. The user now wants rules that override good judgment "gone or fixed".
