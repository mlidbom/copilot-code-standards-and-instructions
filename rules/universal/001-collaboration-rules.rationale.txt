# Provenance

## What is behind each section

- **Don't code until instructed to,** and **honesty about what you don't know:** Compze's own rules, in the catalog
  since 2026-06-12. The second answered an agent that kept the old architecture, said nothing and reported success:
  most likely Opus 4.6, the latest Opus when Compze got the rule, 2026-03-07.
- **Questions are just questions,** and **the codebase must improve over time:** from the user-level CLAUDE.md of
  before the rules folder.
- **Refactorings are the agent's to make** (2026-11-14, Compze, Claude Fable 5.2). The section read "If you touch a file
  and notice something wrong", and the agent took that for the files of its task. The user: "Our leave the code better
  guidelines was intended to already make it clear that, barring changes to the public API's of our published packages,
  clearly beneficial refactorings that you discover, you are intended to just do." The agent proposed where to stop
  ("a new abstraction or a changed relation between subsystems: propose first"), and he answered: "A new abstraction
  could mean small internal types, methods etc. That is most likely perfectly fine and very much in line with the code
  standards. Use judgment, not hard rules. Do notify me about what changed." The other points are the agent's, and he
  agreed to them: what it comes across and not a hunt, commits of their own, everything code outside the repository
  can compile against as the limit, and keeping to the task's files while another session is in the checkout.
- **Build the tools the work will need again now** (2026-11-09, JAStudio). The user: "In the spirit of creating the
  tools we will need again and again right away rather than postponing creating them. Thus speeding up long term
  progress. This is absolutely a basic idea in how I think." And: "I specifically want you to consider and suggest
  major changes if required to speed up future development, not just small additions to existing infrastructure. Not
  least tooling specifically to help YOU or other AI agents. For instance. It took me FAR to long to suggest adding MCP
  to jastudio."
- **The tools are the agent's, as a product** (2026-11-14). JAStudio (2026-11-09), Deskmancer (2026-11-12) and Compze
  (2026-11-14) each had a rule of their own saying nearly the same, the first in the user's words: "I would like to
  give you total product ownership of the MCP. It is YOUR ui into jastudio." What they shared is here now, and each
  project's rule keeps its list of tools and its own limits. "A tool the user runs himself is his too" is his answer
  when the agent asked to own the interfaces of Compze's commands: "Is it enough to be free to add to them, without
  changing the default behaviours, and asking me if you want a tool to, for instance, become more verbose by default?"
- **Commit and push whenever it helps.** Committing: 2026-11-11. Pushing: 2026-11-14, when the agent asked for leave to
  push the branch it was working on, to run CI: "Always OK regardless of branch. Nothing push does is irreversible."
  Asked whether that held in his other repositories: "Yes, now it is, deskmancer no longer publishes on push to main."
- **When given full delegation:** from Compze's separation of typermedia and tessaging, 2026-06-10. The case is in
  `code-standards/014-rehome-concepts-before-restructuring.rationale.txt`.

## Taken out, and not to put back

- **"REFUSE to start work when you lack what you need to succeed"** and **"Ask the design question upfront",** of the
  section then called "Honesty about blockers — MANDATORY". Removed 2026-11-14 (JAStudio, Claude Fable 5.2): they had
  the agent present a question about a name as one it needed answered before a design could go on, against the user's
  independence mandate, a user-level rule. Nothing was put in their place: when to ask is the agent's judgment, and
  what was worth keeping of them is "Name what you don't know".
- **A gate on changes outside the repository** was in these rules until 2026-06-13. Since 2026-06-28 the rule on
  external environments holds that.
- **Pushing needing the user's word.** Removed 2026-11-14. A push to Deskmancer's main used to publish, and no longer
  does.

## Not tested

- Whether agents go back to guessing in silence at an unclear target. Look first at "Name what you don't know", before
  adding a point.
- Whether the freedom to refactor is overdone: refactorings he would have wanted to weigh in on, or too many beside a
  task. Look first at "Judgment, not a size limit" and "What you come across, not a hunt".
- A forced push was not talked about when he approved pushing.
