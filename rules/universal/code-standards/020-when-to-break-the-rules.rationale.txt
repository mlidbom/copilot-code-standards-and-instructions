# Provenance

## What is behind it

Until 2026-11-14 the rule said to ask the user first. It was changed that day in JAStudio, in a session of Claude
Fable 5.2, while going through the shared rules with the user:

- **Asking first stops the work** for a decision the agent can make, and the user can judge afterwards, at the code.
  He had already chosen that way of working for JAStudio's redesign of matching, 2026-11-10: "You are so incredibly
  much faster than me that if we wait for me to catch up progress will slow to a halt."
- **With asking as the only way out, every standard was in effect absolute.**

The rule's two examples are the shapes of the exceptions approved under the old rule:

- **Compze:** a raw `Monitor` in `DoubleCheckedLockingOnAnExistingObject`, with a comment and a document, and a
  `SemaphoreSlim` in `FramedConnectionPool`, with a comment only. Both against the standard on locking.
- **JAStudio:** `InternalsVisibleTo` for the specification projects, with a document.

## What looks removable but isn't

- **"Not for a standard you would simply have written differently".** Without it, "defaults" lets back in what the
  standards are there to keep out: short names, defensive checks, no extraction until something is reused.
- **The `//rationale:` comment.** A `review:` comment is an item to resolve. Once it's resolved, the reason has to stay
  at the code, or the next agent corrects the exception.

## Not tested

- **Whether agents now make exceptions too readily.** Look first at the word "defaults".
- **An exception also raises `### A RULE GOT IN THE WAY ###`,** as the two rules are worded. Left so: exceptions are
  rare, and the heading tells the user that a standard didn't fit.
