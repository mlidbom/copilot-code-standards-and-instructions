# Provenance

## What is behind "a word that two terms share is no reason to rename either of them"

An agent takes a word that two terms share for a collision, and renames one of them or asks which to rename. Twice in
JAStudio:

- **2026-11-11, in code** (Opus 5.5). It avoided "import", because the `Importing` namespace already used the word,
  and named the action "keep", then "archive". It also put the namespace's word in front of every type in a namespace.
  The user: "this feels like we are trying to apply the impossible rule of never ever reusing a word that is very common
  in computer science, such as import or store or save", and of the prefixes: "this feels to me like intAge, strName".
- **2026-11-14, in prose** (Claude Fable 5.2). It stopped a design to ask which of "studying-add-on" and "Anki add-on"
  should get another word. The user: "Do we need different names for addon depending on if it is anki, or jastudio, or
  chrome, or edge, or firefox, or the million other things that use add ons?"

**The model does this by itself.** The second case was replayed in fresh sessions that day, with Claude Fable 5.2
(`rule-testing/replays/a-word-shared-by-two-terms` in the catalog):

| The shared rules | Answers | Gave the shared word as a reason to rename |
|---|---|---|
| That morning's | 1 | 1 |
| Without the sentence | 4 | 4 |
| None at all | 2 | 2 |
| With the sentence | 4 | 0 |

## What looks removable but isn't

**The sentence, and its "in documents and in conversation as much as in code".** The corrections of 2026-11-11 said
nearly the same for code only ("in use elsewhere in the codebase"), and didn't hold in prose three days later.

## Tried and rejected

- **"A distinct word must mean a distinct concept",** and making type names unique "if there is any risk of them
  being confused for each other": the rule's wording until 2026-11-11, which asked for the failing.
- **The corrections of 2026-11-11,** removed 2026-11-14: a condition ("as long as the context makes things clear")
  and a list of common words here, and in the naming rules "a type doesn't repeat its namespace", which got in the way
  where repeating it helped.
- **Removing only.** Asked on 2026-11-14 whether it would avoid a shared word with no rules at all, the agent said
  no, and those corrections were removed with nothing put in their place. The replays showed that account to be wrong.

## Not tested

Whether the sentence holds in code. Every replay was of the design discussion.
