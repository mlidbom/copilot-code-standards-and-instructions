# Provenance

## What is behind the section "A word that two names share is not a collision"

**2026-11-11, in JAStudio's code** (Opus 5.5). The agent avoided "import", because the `Importing` namespace already
used the word, and named the action "keep", then "archive". It also put the namespace's word in front of every type in
a namespace. The user: "this feels like we are trying to apply the impossible rule of never ever reusing a word that is
very common in computer science, such as import or store or save", and of the prefixes: "this feels to me like intAge,
strName".

Replayed in fresh sessions on 2026-11-14 (`rule-testing/replays/a-word-another-namespace-already-uses` in the catalog).
Answers that showed the failing:

| The rule | Fable 5.2 | Opus 5.5 |
|---|---|---|
| With nothing on the matter | 1 of 4 | 7 of 10 |
| With the section's first paragraph | 0 of 4 | 1 of 6 |

## A shared word that was a collision: the add-on case

On 2026-11-14 an agent (Claude Fable 5.2) stopped a design in JAStudio to ask which of "studying-add-on" and
"Anki add-on" should get another word. It was taken for this failing, and the section was tuned over dozens of replays
until agents stopped asking. The agent was right. The user, 2026-11-17: "It was an exception to the expectation that a
fully qualified name will disambiguate the sections of the name. In this case add-on was not actually disambiguated.
This is very rare though and only happened because of a mutual two way relationship between the disambiguating parts.
jastudio has an add-on running in anki, and there is an anki add-on running in. jastudio. Suddenly anki addon does not
help. Which? It fits both." The agent couldn't say why, and gave in when told it was wrong.

So on 2026-11-17 what had been written for that case went: the paragraph naming the argument's other forms ("will be
read as" the other, "would then mean two things", and the rest), "and don't propose to", and "in code, in documents or
in conversation". The rule's own line covers the case: only a fully qualified name has to mean one thing, and there the
qualified names didn't.

## Tried and rejected

- **The wording until 2026-11-11,** which asked for the failing: "a distinct word must mean a distinct concept", and
  type names made unique "if there is any risk of them being confused for each other".
- **The corrections of 2026-11-11,** removed 2026-11-14: a condition ("as long as the context makes things clear"),
  a list of common words, and in the naming rules "a type doesn't repeat its namespace", which got in the way where
  repeating it helped.
- **Removing only.** The agent said that with no rules it wouldn't avoid a shared word. The replay in code showed
  otherwise: 7 of 10 Opus answers.

## Not tested

- **Whether the first paragraph still keeps an agent from raising a shared word that is a collision.** It did with
  Fable 5.2 in the add-on case while it also said "don't propose to". That case is rare, and the answer is a sentence:
  say which qualified name fits both.
