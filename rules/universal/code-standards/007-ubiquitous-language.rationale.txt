# Provenance

## What is behind "the same word can mean different things in different contexts"

The agent took the rule to mean that a word may mean only one thing in the whole codebase. Twice, both in JAStudio:

- **2026-11-11, in code** (the model isn't recorded). It avoided "import", because the `Importing` namespace already
  used the word, and named the action "keep", then "archive". It also put the namespace's word in front of every type
  in a namespace. The user: "this feels like we are trying to apply the impossible rule of never ever reusing a word
  that is very common in computer science, such as import or store or save", and of the prefixes: "this feels to me
  like intAge, strName".
- **2026-11-14, in prose** (Claude Fable 5.2). It stopped a design to ask which of "studying-add-on" and "Anki
  add-on" should get another word. The user: "Do we need different names for addon depending on if it is anki, or
  jastudio, or chrome, or edge, or firefox, or the million other things that use add ons?"

The agent's account of its own part: it finds code by searching text, so a name unique across the codebase suits it,
at the human reader's cost. Asked whether it would avoid a shared word with no rules at all, it said no.

## Taken out, and not to put back

- **"A distinct word must mean a distinct concept",** and making type names unique "if there is any risk of them
  being confused for each other": the wording the agent read that way. Out 2026-11-11.
- **The corrections of 2026-11-11,** out again 2026-11-14: here, a condition ("as long as the context makes things
  clear") and a list of common words; in the naming rules, "a type doesn't repeat its namespace", which got in the way
  where repeating it helped, and "carry the disambiguating qualifier" with an exception. Each was a new absolute, was
  worded for code only, and left the decision to a judgment the naming rules told the agent not to trust.

The user, on choosing to remove: "we may need to look at just removing parts of the current instructions rather than
changing them to be ever more complex."
