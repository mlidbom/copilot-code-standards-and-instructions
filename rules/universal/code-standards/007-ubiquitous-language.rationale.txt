# Provenance

## What is behind the section "A word that two names share is not a collision"

An agent takes a word that is part of two names for a collision, and renames one of them or asks which to rename.
Twice in JAStudio:

- **2026-11-11, in code** (Opus 5.5). It avoided "import", because the `Importing` namespace already used the word,
  and named the action "keep", then "archive". It also put the namespace's word in front of every type in a namespace.
  The user: "this feels like we are trying to apply the impossible rule of never ever reusing a word that is very common
  in computer science, such as import or store or save", and of the prefixes: "this feels to me like intAge, strName".
- **2026-11-14, in prose** (Claude Fable 5.2). It stopped a design to ask which of "studying-add-on" and "Anki add-on"
  should get another word. The user: "Do we need different names for addon depending on if it is anki, or jastudio, or
  chrome, or edge, or firefox, or the million other things that use add ons?"

**The model does this by itself, and the section stops only one of the two models tried.** The second case was
replayed in fresh sessions that day (`rule-testing/replays/a-word-shared-by-two-terms` in the catalog). Answers that
gave the shared word as a reason to rename:

| The shared rules | Claude Fable 5.2 | Opus 5.5 |
|---|---|---|
| None at all | 2 of 2 | not run |
| With nothing on the matter | 3 of 3 | 4 of 4 |
| With a sentence on it inside the paragraph | 13 of 13, in three wordings | 4 of 4 |
| With the section | 0 of 7 | 4 of 4 |

**For Opus 5.5 the rule does not yet work.** Opus is the model the user works with most.

## What looks removable but isn't

- **The heading of its own.** Said as a sentence inside the paragraph above it, the same thing changed nothing: "A word
  that two terms share is no reason to rename either of them", nor with "a word that is the last part of", or "part
  of", "two different fully qualified names".
- **"And don't propose to", and "in code, in documents or in conversation".** The corrections of 2026-11-11 spoke of
  code only ("in use elsewhere in the codebase"), and didn't hold in prose three days later.
- **The examples are not the replay's own.** With "add-on" as the example, the replay would test nothing.

## Tried and rejected

- **"A distinct word must mean a distinct concept",** and making type names unique "if there is any risk of them
  being confused for each other": the rule's wording until 2026-11-11, which asked for the failing.
- **The corrections of 2026-11-11,** removed 2026-11-14: a condition ("as long as the context makes things clear")
  and a list of common words here, and in the naming rules "a type doesn't repeat its namespace", which got in the way
  where repeating it helped.
- **Removing only.** Asked on 2026-11-14 whether it would avoid a shared word with no rules at all, the agent said
  no, and those corrections were removed with nothing put in their place. The replays showed that account to be wrong.
- **The sentence inside the paragraph,** committed twice that day on a flawed test: 10 answers of 10 had seemed to
  show it working. Those sessions ran in folders named after what was tested, such as `with-the-sentence`, and a
  session is told its working directory. In folders with plain names the sentence did nothing.

## Not tested

- **Whether the section holds in code.** Every replay was of the design discussion.
- **Two of the seven answers still preferred "studying integration"**, for another reason: that Anki isn't added on to
  JAStudio. That is a view on whether the name tells the truth, which the naming rule asks for.
