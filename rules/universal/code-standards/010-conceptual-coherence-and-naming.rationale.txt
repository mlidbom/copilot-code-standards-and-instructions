# Provenance

## 2026-11-14 (JAStudio): five files became this one

Until then the catalog had five always-on files on conceptual coherence and naming, 1,528 words together:
`010-conceptual-coherence-and-naming.md`, `011-naming-and-conceptual-fit-are-preeminent.md`,
`012-renaming-is-the-most-important-refactoring.md`, `013-name-for-the-human-who-must-look-it-up.md` and
`032-naming.md`. Each was written for its own incident, in June 2026, and together they said most things several times:

- that renaming is the most important refactoring: 010, 012 and 032;
- that a name is as long as it must be, and brevity only a tie-breaker: 010, 013 and 032;
- the `User` example: 010 and 032;
- that the name is the concept's one word everywhere: all five.

They were merged while going through the shared rules with the user (`fixing-bad-rules-worklist.txt` in the catalog's
`rules` folder, while that work lasts), in a session of Claude Fable 5.2. The step was: "the five naming files into
one, in Magnus's words". The result is 785 words.

- **Each thing is said once,** and what another rule says is linked: that a rename applies everywhere
  (`007-ubiquitous-language.md`), extracting whatever the reuse (`040-standard-wisdom-traps.md`), how a method reads
  (`031-everything-in-its-place.md`).
- **The user's own words are quoted** where the sidecars below record them. Wording carried over from the old files
  without quotation marks may be his too: which sentences he wrote himself isn't recorded.
- **What told the agent not to trust its own reading is gone.** From 013: the title's "your own comprehension is a
  broken gauge"; "Two forces push you the wrong way. Treat both as suspect signals, not as taste"; "The urge to shorten
  is a training artifact to resist, not an instinct to trust"; "self-comprehension is worthless as a quality check".
  Sentences of that kind are what had the agent look for ambiguities nobody had met
  (`007-ubiquitous-language.rationale.txt`, 2026-11-14). What the reader needs is said instead, in the user's words.
  His sentence about the code agents are trained on is kept as what it is: his taste.
- **The force is taken down to plain statement.** Gone: "The king of all concerns", "not polish — it is a fatal design
  error", "This file only ranks it: it outranks everything below", "Catching yourself listing conceptual fit as one
  consideration in a balanced trade-off means you have already lost the thread", "Never the reverse; never recommend
  against the coherent model on pragmatic grounds". The ranking itself stays: in the title, and in the user's "absolute
  world champion of importance".
- **The reader is someone new to the code, not to its domain.** 013 came from a project whose domains were new to the
  user, where the agent had written nearly all the code. Its "you already know the domain, the APIs, the surrounding
  code" went with the paragraph it was in.
- **Dropped:** "Keep distinct domain words distinct (a `chord` is the key combination; a `hotkey` is the bound,
  globally-caught action)", which `007-ubiquitous-language.md` covers; and 011's "a constant, major source of
  misunderstanding", which is in its sidecar below.
- **One term:** the old files said "conceptual coherence" and "conceptual fit" for the same thing. The rule now says
  "conceptual coherence"; "fit" is left where the user's own words have it.

## Not tested, and what to watch for

- **Whether names now come out shorter, or less telling.** If so, look first at "As long as it takes for a reader new
  to the code to understand it with no lookup": the old wording, in the sidecar of 013 below and in the catalog's
  history, pushed much harder.
- **Whether the agent goes back to answering a question about a name with how the implementation works.** That's the
  failing 011 was written for, twice in a row, by a model that isn't recorded.

## What the five files' own sidecars said

Kept as they were, under the name of the file each belonged to. `032-naming.md` had none.

### `010-conceptual-coherence-and-naming.md`

Written 2026-06-05 in the vdgs-live-view project; supersedes the earlier
understand-code-by-names-not-implementations rule, renamed so the rule's own name tells the truth about its
now-broader scope — the very principle it states.

Origin: the project's top-priority rule led with "Simple, maintainable code is the most important thing."
Asked whether a `record struct Monitor` was the right model, the agent collapsed "simple/maintainable" to the
*median* meaning — minimalism / YAGNI / "don't abstract until reused" — and argued to keep the value type on
implementation-usage grounds. The opposite of what the user means.

The fix dropped the word "simple" entirely (redefining a common word just re-collapses to the median every
time the definition isn't in front of the reader) and stated the standard operationally: code is understood
by reading names, never by decoding implementations. The user then raised that *type* names matter as much as
member names — a class/interface name that implies something untrue is a fatal design error, not polish — and
that this is really about conceptual coherence, of which naming is a large part. Hence the rule's scope and
name broadened to conceptual coherence.

Much of the read-by-names content already lived in the everything-in-its-place / naming rules; what was
missing at the priority level was naming the standard without the misleading word, enumerating
minimalism/YAGNI and short-names as traps, and putting type-name truthfulness up front.

Standing standard for every project, hence user-level despite duplicating the project's. The user explicitly
accepted that duplication for something this vital.

### `011-naming-and-conceptual-fit-are-preeminent.md`

vdgs-live-view (Vantage), 2026-06-05. Vantage.Geometry/Monitor.cs:
`public readonly record struct Monitor(ScreenRect Bounds)`.

The user asked whether `record struct` / the name `Monitor` was a good conceptual fit: a monitor is
an entity (identity, DPI, refresh rate, mutable config, behavior), not a value object — does it even
belong in Geometry?

#### The failure (committed twice in a row), then the broader correction
Both first replies rebutted the CONCEPTUAL critique with IMPLEMENTATION facts: "it's read fresh as a
snapshot, value-equality is load-bearing (`MonitorOf(window) == monitor`), so the record/value
design is correct and the name is fine." The user called this out: looking at deep implementation
details and concluding "X invalidates your question," when the question was about conceptual design
and naming. "If the name it is given is messed up given what it is used as, that does NOT mean it is
OK — it means we have a fundamental modeling error to fix."

Then he generalized it into a standing value ranking (this is the heart of the rule):
- "The pre-eminence of naming and conceptual fit."
- "Implementation details are a distant, also-ran, in importance to me, while clear intuitive mental
  models are the absolute world champion of importance."
- "When I'm pointing out something about code, unless I'm specifically pointing at a bug, the odds
  are 90%+ that what I'm talking about is questionable design in this sense, not implementation."
- My tendency to focus ~99% on implementation and treat naming/mental-model coherence as not
  terribly important is "a major constant source of misunderstandings."

So the rule is a PRIOR on interpretation + a value ranking, not just a "don't do X" — read his
remarks as design/coherence first; rank conceptual fit above implementation by default.

#### The actual answer once engaged correctly (kept for the worked example)
`Monitor` the name promises a physical display device (entity); the thing is the rectangular AREA a
monitor covers (a geometric value, compared interchangeably). Name says entity, thing is a region —
that dissonance IS the defect. Coherent fixes: (1) name it for the value it is — `MonitorArea`,
stays in Geometry; (2) make it a real device entity in Domain (identity, DPI, refresh, behavior);
(3) split the conflation — a `Monitor` entity (Domain) that HAS a `MonitorArea` value (Geometry),
reserving the name `Monitor` for the device. Which one is the user's call (depends on whether
monitors should grow behavior), not something to settle on implementation grounds.

#### Why a user-level rule, not a project memory
Per ~/.claude/rules/where-to-store-lessons-learned.md: this is a cross-project value ranking and
interpretation prior (how the user thinks and what he weights in ANY codebase), not a
vdgs-live-view fact. Lean always-on .md + this sidecar. Renamed from the narrower
engage-conceptual-critique-not-implementation.* because the user broadened it from a reactive
"don't explain away" rule to the general preeminence of naming/mental models.

### `012-renaming-is-the-most-important-refactoring.md`

Written 2026-06-05 in the vdgs-live-view project. The user stated, verbatim, that he "100% seriously
consider[s] renaming to be the most important refactoring that exists" — captured here because it is a
standing, cross-project conviction, not a one-off remark.

It crystallised from the `Monitor` case. A type named `Monitor` (which implies the physical-display entity)
actually held only a `ScreenRect` and was used purely geometrically. Two wrong fixes were weighed first: keep
the struct and defend the name by its usage (defending a lie with implementation), and convert it to an entity
class (building a concept the app does not have). The right fix was the rename — it *is* a value (a region),
so name it as one. The rename resolved the design error; nothing else had to change. That is the pattern the
rule generalises: a name carries the abstraction, so fixing the name is usually the fix itself.

### `013-name-for-the-human-who-must-look-it-up.md`

Written 2026-06-08 in the Virtual-Desktop-Grid-Switcher (Vantage) project, during a naming-harmonization pass
that renamed the hotkey subsystem (`Hotkeys` -> `GlobalHotkeys`, `_hotkeys` -> `_globalHotkeys`,
`GlobalShortcutGroup` -> `GlobalHotkeyGroup`, and so on). Unprompted, the user named the two forces this rule
generalises:

  1. "You are trained on heaps and heaps of code of a type that I utterly hate that optimizes for characters
     typed and just loves variable names like a,b,c,d and ad-hoc abbreviations and acronyms."

  2. "You know everything (compared to me). When you see a symbol, you know so much about what it is ... that it
     could probably be named anything and the code would not become THAT much harder to understand to you. To
     me, without a great name, I need to go looking things up CONSTANTLY to be sure what I'm looking at."

Why this earns its own rule rather than folding into 010-012: those assert that naming and conceptual fit are
the top concern and that renaming is the highest-leverage refactoring — the *importance*. This one diagnoses
the *mechanism* behind the agent's repeated under-naming, which is the actionable part: the agent's own
comprehension never degrades on a bad name, so it is a broken gauge, and the agent must (a) switch the quality
test to the human-who-does-not-already-know and (b) treat the pull toward brevity as a training artifact, not
taste.

Immediate trigger: the agent had shortened the field to `_hotkeys` and the class to `Hotkeys`; the user
insisted on the fully-qualified `_globalHotkeys` / `GlobalHotkeys`, with: "make the naming universal. not
hotkeys, globalHotkeys. Do not fear typing. Readability and being unambiguous is king." The agent had also
initially argued *for* dropping the "Global" qualifier as redundant (because "hotkey" already implies global)
— a textbook instance of force #2: redundant to the agent, load-bearing for the human.

#### Amended 2026-11-11 (JAStudio): a qualifier the context already gives is the exception

The agent applied "carry the disambiguating qualifier even when it feels redundant" where the namespace already said it,
prefixing every type in a format namespace (`Archiving.Format.ArchivedNoteLine` and the like); the user called it
`strName` again. So the rule now excepts a qualifier the name's own context gives, and 032-naming says a type doesn't
repeat its namespace. The `GlobalHotkeys` case stands: there, "global" was in no context the reader saw, only implied by
the word "hotkey" to the agent. See 007-ubiquitous-language.rationale.txt for the whole episode.

#### Amended 2026-11-14 (JAStudio): the pushes toward ever more qualifiers taken out

Removed: "Deliberately over-correct toward the explicit, unambiguous name; do not trust the green light your own
understanding gives"; "Carry the disambiguating qualifier even when it feels redundant to you", with the exception of
2026-11-11; "rather than collapsing them because you can tell which is meant from context"; and "alone" and "on its own".
They had the agent (Claude Fable 5.2) treat any word shared between two terms as an ambiguity to remove, for an imagined
reader, whatever its own reading said. Of the rule itself, and the others written in Deskmancer in June, Magnus said that
day: "That was in the opus 4.6 era." The `GlobalHotkeys` case above stands as history: what remains of the rule, a name as long as it
must be to be understood with no lookup, covers it. See 007-ubiquitous-language.rationale.txt for the whole episode.
